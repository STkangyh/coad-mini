02

Invariance to increment size

Splitting the same classes into smaller sessions moves every other method. This one does not move.

At every class count that two or more schedules share (51, 61, 71, 81, 91 and 101 classes), accuracy is bit-identical across all three schedules and all three seeds. Final accuracy at 101 classes is 88.61 in every case. The remaining −0.03pp in Accavg comes only from the extra evaluation points a finer schedule adds: 6, 11 and 26 of them.

The head only accumulates per-class means and a shared covariance, and those statistics are the same whatever order or grouping the samples arrive in. A user can teach a device new actions one at a time and lose nothing.

03

Keeping frame order

Mean pooling cannot tell “push left to right” from “push right to left”. Segment means can.

Something-Something V2 has many classes that differ only in the order things happen, and averaging the frames erases that order. Splitting the window into contiguous segments and concatenating their means, as drawn above, recovers much of it with zero parameters and zero gradient steps.

  • Order is the cause, not dimension. mean+std, which ignores order, adds only 1.04pp.
  • It does not depend on the head. NCM gains 6.04, SLDA 8.36 and FeCAM 8.97pp on the 48-class subset.
  • The gain is small where order matters little. UCF101, mostly decided by appearance, gains 1.16pp.
  • It holds at full scale. On SSv2's standard 174-class split final accuracy rises 6.77pp and stays invariant to the schedule.
How sure we are

Choosing the variant on held-out training data instead of validation gives +7.83pp rather than +8.97pp, and the top variants are within seed noise of each other. The defensible claim is that any order-preserving pooling adds 7 to 9pp over mean pooling. The live demo serves chunks4, the variant that held-out selection picked.

04

Where it stops working

On appearance-defined actions it sits just under a jointly trained probe. On motion-defined actions a trained temporal encoder is still far ahead.

On SSv2's 174-class split, ESSENTIAL (ICCV’25) reaches 48.9% average incremental accuracy against our 21.17%. Its trained temporal encoder extracts motion information that closed-form pooling does not. In exchange we store no exemplars, never run backprop, and keep an 8.17 MiB head checkpoint.

06

How the project got here

Jun 2026

GRU + A-GEM on a 48-class SSv2 subset

Replay with A-GEM beat a sequential baseline, but capacity, a bigger backbone, attention and SSMs did not help further. Only more data did.

Jul 2026

Dropping backprop

Closed-form heads (NCM, SLDA, FeCAM) on frozen CLIP features beat GRU + A-GEM on every metric. The served model switched to FeCAM, and UCF101 under the TCD protocol and CIFAR-100 under PyCIL confirmed it outside our own benchmark.

Aug 2026

Order-preserving pooling and real video

Segment pooling recovered 7 to 9pp on SSv2. The full SSv2 release replaced proxy estimates with measurements, including the 174-class split and live-window tests.

Sep–Oct 2026

Verification

Every comparison number was re-checked against its source paper, the selection protocol was tightened, and the invariance claim was narrowed to what the raw results prove.

The June-era write-ups are kept for history: data scale, GRU + attention, early research log. The full dated record, corrections included, is reports/RESEARCH_LOG.md.

07

Run it

The demo server streams predictions from an uploaded video or a webcam, lets you enrol a new action from a few clips, and flags out-of-distribution windows. It runs on the included checkpoints.

pip install -r requirements.txt
uvicorn app:app --port 8000          # or: docker build -t coad-mini . && docker run -p 7860:7860 coad-mini
  • POST /predict_rt realtime prediction on a frame window
  • POST /enroll add a new action class from a few example videos (details)
  • POST /predict_anomaly prediction plus an out-of-distribution score (details)
